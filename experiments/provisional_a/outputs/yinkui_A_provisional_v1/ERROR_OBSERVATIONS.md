# Initial qualitative error observations

Assistant inspection of full cleaned text, removed passages and fixed-C validation predictions. These are hypotheses for the team's review, not adjudication or proof of a model's internal reasoning. No labels or masks were changed after looking at predictions. Class 0 means affirmed; class 1 means reversed/vacated, not plaintiff success.

| Case | Observation | Interpretation to investigate |
|---|---|---|
| NY-7855719, Schneider v. Gap | All three models match the provisional reversal label on FULL, but predict affirmed on MASKED. Masking removes the operative reversal and the final should-have-granted sentence. It retains surveillance evidence that the plaintiff tripped over her own feet, alongside the earlier trial denial. | The reversal favors the defendant. The remaining merits discussion resembles an affirmance of a defense victory even though the actual earlier order denied the defense motion. This is a concrete example where reading merits alone is insufficient: the appealed ruling and appellate result must be related correctly. It does not establish that all models internally made this exact error. |
| NY-4929051, Zubillaga | LR and SVM predict affirmed on both versions. LEGAL-BERT matches reversal on FULL but predicts affirmed on MASKED. This is the longest validation opinion (1,796 cleaned words). Both opening reversed orders are removed; a long procedural history, original default ruling, leave-to-appeal discussion and merits analysis remain. | Multiple orders and procedural history can compete with the merits signal. The complete opinion is chunked, so this is not an error caused by discarding everything after token 512. Hayden should confirm the eligibility of this procedural-plus-merits appeal. |
| NY-7857572, Yerry | Both TF-IDF models predict affirmed on both versions; LEGAL-BERT matches reversal on both. The reversal concerns failure to eliminate notice/remedial-measure issues. The mask also removes an evidentiary approval sentence unrelated to the overall reversal. | Preserving the merits discussion can still be useful to a model, but lexical baselines may confuse earlier summary judgment with the appellate result. This case also illustrates collateral loss from the current broad masking rule. |
| NY-9355453, Cheprakova | LR and SVM match affirmed on both versions. LEGAL-BERT matches on FULL but predicts reversed on MASKED. The remaining reasoning says the plaintiff could not identify the cause of the fall; precedents and surveillance discussion remain. | A masked model can fail despite useful reasoning being retained. The mask also deletes one redundant historical ruling because “Inc.” affects sentence boundaries. This is not a clean outcome-only intervention. |

Training-derived LR coefficients provide another diagnostic: FULL includes strong class associations for “affirmed,” “reversed,” and “court properly.” In MASKED, department/citation-style terms such as “1st dept” and content terms such as “rug” and “trivial” remain influential. This raises a possible writing-style/case-mix confound; coefficients alone do not prove why an individual prediction occurred. Department references inside citations do not necessarily identify the deciding department.

For a final report, the team should review these examples and expand the gallery as the dataset grows. The full 16-case validation packet is provided so errors are not selected only to favor one model. Keep label adjudication separate: Hayden should use the original annotations and opinions without these model predictions until gold labels are frozen.
